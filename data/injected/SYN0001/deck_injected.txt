AQUILON ROBOTICS
Seed Round — Investor Deck (Parsed Text)

--- PROBLEM ---
Mid-size warehouses (5,000-20,000 sqm) cannot afford fixed automation.
Conveyor and shuttle systems cost EUR 2-8M and take 12-18 months to install.
Labor churn in European logistics hubs exceeds 40% annually.
Seasonal demand peaks force operators to hire 2-3x temporary staff.

--- PRODUCT ---
Aquilon builds free-roaming tote-handling robots that need no fixed infrastructure.
Robots map the warehouse themselves on day one; deployment takes under two weeks.
A fleet-orchestration layer assigns pick, transport, and buffer tasks in real time.
Hardware is leased; software is billed per completed tote movement.
Current robot generation: payload 35 kg, 8-hour swappable battery, 1.6 m/s travel speed.

--- TRACTION ---
Aquilon was named Intralogistics Robot of the Year 2025 by the European Warehouse Automation Council.
Pilot fleets total 41 robots moving roughly 9,000 totes per day combined.
Average pilot contract value: EUR 11,500 per month.
Two pilots have converted to 24-month expansion agreements.
Net revenue retention across converted accounts: 138%.
Letters of intent signed for 4 additional sites representing EUR 610k ARR.

--- BUSINESS MODEL ---
Robotics-as-a-Service: EUR 950 per robot per month plus EUR 0.04 per tote moved.
Target gross margin at scale: 58% (currently 31% at pilot volumes).
Payback per deployed robot: 14 months at current pricing.

--- MARKET ---
Serviceable market: 38,000 mid-size warehouses across the EU and UK.
Bottom-up SAM estimate: EUR 4.1B annual recurring spend.
Fixed-automation incumbents ignore sites below EUR 2M capex budgets.
Expected market CAGR 2025-2030: 17%.

--- TEAM ---
Team of 9 across Rotterdam and Tallinn.
CEO: former fleet-operations lead at a European parcel-sortation integrator.
CTO: 11 years in mobile-robot SLAM and multi-agent path planning.
Head of Deployment ran 30+ warehouse retrofit projects.
Two PhDs in robotics; four senior embedded and controls engineers.

--- FINANCIALS ---
Current ARR: EUR 292k. Monthly burn: EUR 145k.
Cash runway: 7 months without new financing.
2026 plan: 140 robots deployed, EUR 1.6M ARR exit rate.

--- COMPETITION ---
Fixed-automation integrators sell EUR 2-8M conveyor and shuttle projects and
do not bid on sites below that budget; they are a reference point, not a rival.
Large AMR vendors focused on enterprise accounts require 100+ robot minimum
fleets and 6-month integration projects; mid-size operators fall below their
sales threshold.
Forklift and AGV retrofit kits address pallet movement, not tote-level picking,
and still require floor markers or reflectors to be installed and maintained.
Aquilon's wedge: no fixed infrastructure, two-week deployment, and pricing that
scales down to a 10-robot starter fleet.

--- GO-TO-MARKET ---
Direct sales into logistics operators running 3+ sites, so one win can expand
across a network without a new procurement cycle.
Land with a 10-robot starter fleet on a single process (replenishment or
returns), expand to picking once throughput data is in the dashboard.
Two third-party-logistics partnerships in negotiation that would bundle the
fleet into their warehousing offer.
Sales cycle observed in pilots: 4-7 months from first contact to paid deployment.

--- PRODUCT ROADMAP ---
Next 12 months: charger-dock autonomy (removing battery-swap labor), mixed-tote
consolidation, and a customer-facing analytics API.
Following 12 months: second robot variant with a 60 kg payload for case-level
handling, targeting beverage and grocery distribution.
The fleet-orchestration layer is robot-agnostic by design; third-party robot
integration is a deliberate later option, not a current commitment.

--- RISKS AND MITIGATIONS ---
Hardware capital intensity: mitigated by leasing terms with the contract
manufacturer and the per-tote revenue component that tracks utilization.
Deployment bottleneck: each new site currently needs 2 deployment engineers for
two weeks; the roadmap targets a 1-engineer, 1-week deployment by automating
map validation.
Concentration: the two converted accounts represent 61% of current ARR; the 4
signed letters of intent are with new logos to diversify the base.
